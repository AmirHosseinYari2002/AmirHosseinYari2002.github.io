01

What counts as one segment?

One segment = one explicit local operation

Keep the operation together with its necessary lead-in, operands, and stated result. It can depend on earlier context, span several sentences, or occupy only part of a sentence.

Insert CUT when a distinct new introduction, inference, calculation, check, correction, or method choice begins. Follow the operation being expressed, even if its content is incorrect.

Apply the same rule in every case

Several sentences, one operation
Keep together. Sentence boundaries do not determine segments.
One sentence, distinct operations
Split at the new operation. A check followed by a separate calculation needs a boundary.
Calculation + its stated result
Keep together. The result completes that calculation.
Two calculations on the same topic
Split if they are distinct operations. A shared topic or action type does not merge them.
A lead-in or an explicit replacement
Keep the lead-in with its content and the replacement with its value. Avoid unfinished fragments.
Punctuation, a newline, or a long passage
Do not split for formatting or length alone. Look for a distinct operation inside the passage.
Uncertain boundary
Flag the character position and describe both interpretations. Do not invent an unstated operation.

Start a new operation

Insert CUT before a distinct introduction, inference, calculation, check, correction, or method choice.

Keep the operation together

Keep a lead-in with its content, a calculation with its stated result, and a replacement with its replacement value.

Punctuation, a newline, or length alone is not a reason to split. A shared topic or action category is not a reason to merge distinct operations.

03

A small worked example

Input · Unsegmented source

Compute u = 2 + 3. The result is u = 5. Check: u − 3 = 2, then compute v: v = 2u = 10.

Task A · Independent segmentation

Compute u = 2 + 3. The result is u = 5. CUT Check: u − 3 = 2, CUT then compute v: v = 2u = 10.

The calculation stays with its result. The check and the next calculation begin separate operations. This website collects Task A boundaries only; action labels are not requested.
